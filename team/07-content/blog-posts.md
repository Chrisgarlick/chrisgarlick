---

AI implementation for a law firm means identifying the specific tasks your staff repeat every week — document drafting, client intake, internal reporting — and building systems that handle them automatically. It does not mean installing a chatbot on your website. It does not mean buying a subscription to an AI product and hoping someone uses it. It means auditing your operations, scoping the workflows that waste the most fee-earning time, and building targeted automations that run reliably. A typical engagement takes 2–4 weeks and costs £5,000–£8,000 at a fixed fee.

Most firms I speak to have heard about AI from the Law Society newsletter, a conference panel, or a LinkedIn post. They know it matters. They do not know where to start. This post breaks down what implementation actually involves, step by step, with real numbers.

### Key Takeaways

- AI implementation for law firms starts with an operations audit, not a technology purchase.
- The highest-impact workflows to automate first are document drafting, client intake, and internal reporting.
- A typical build takes 2–4 weeks at a fixed fee of £5,000–£8,000.
- Junior associates in a 15-person firm spend roughly 40% of their time on templated documents. That is the first target.
- Maintenance matters as much as the build. A monthly retainer keeps systems running and improving.

### What "Implementation" Actually Means

AI implementation is the process of identifying manual workflows in your firm, designing AI-powered replacements, building those replacements, and maintaining them over time. It is not a product you install. It is not a platform you subscribe to. It is a set of systems designed around how your firm actually operates.

The word "implementation" gets used loosely. Vendors use it to mean "setting up our software." I use it to mean: audit your operations, find the bottlenecks, build the automations, hand them over with documentation, and maintain them monthly.

### Where Law Firms Waste the Most Time

I have audited enough firms to see the same patterns. Three areas consistently account for the most wasted hours.

**1. Document drafting.** Junior associates spend 40% or more of their week producing templated documents. Engagement letters, NDAs, standard correspondence, first drafts of contracts. These documents follow predictable structures. An AI system drafts them in minutes using your firm's existing templates and client data.

**2. Client intake.** The average firm I work with requires 5–7 touchpoints before a new matter begins — emails, calls, forms, follow-ups, conflict checks, engagement letters. An automated intake system reduces that to 2–3 touchpoints, often same-day.

**3. Internal reporting.** Fee-earners tracking time, practice managers compiling utilisation reports, partners reviewing financial summaries. Manual data extraction and formatting eats hours every week. AI pulls the data, formats it, and delivers the report on schedule.

### What a Real Engagement Looks Like

Every engagement I run follows three phases.

**Phase 1: Audit (week 1).** I run a Kritano audit on your firm's digital presence and operations. Kritano is a site intelligence platform I built. It identifies gaps in technical performance, accessibility, and AI visibility. Alongside that, I map your internal workflows — who does what, how long it takes, where the repetition sits. The output is a report showing exactly which tasks are worth automating and the expected time savings.

**Phase 2: Build (weeks 2–3).** I build the workflows. Document automation connected to your templates. Intake system connected to your case management software. Reporting pipelines pulling from your existing data sources. Everything is scoped, documented, and built to integrate with the tools you already use — Clio, Microsoft 365, whatever your stack is.

**Phase 3: Handover and maintain (week 4 onwards).** I hand over the system with full documentation and run a training session with your staff. From there, a monthly retainer covers maintenance, new workflow additions, and ROI reporting. You get a monthly summary showing what the system handled and how much time it saved.

### How Much Does It Cost?

The Build costs £5,000–£8,000. That is a fixed fee for the full scope — audit, build, handover. No day rates. No open-ended discovery phases.

For a 15-person firm billing £2.5M per year, recovering 15 hours per week of fee-earning time represents tens of thousands in annual value.

### What AI Cannot Do for Your Firm

It will not replace qualified legal judgement. Document automation handles the structure and first draft. A solicitor still reviews, edits, and signs off.

It will not process sensitive data without proper scoping. Every system I build is designed around specific data flows — specific documents, specific fields, specific outputs.

It will not work if your staff do not use it. That is why the handover phase includes training, and the retainer includes ongoing support.

### What About Data Security?

Every automation I build is targeted. Specific documents. Specific workflows. Specific outputs. I do not build systems that access your entire network. Data handling is documented in the architecture, and you review it before anything goes live.

## Post 2: The 3 Workflows Every Agency Should Automate First

---

The three workflows every agency should automate first are client reporting, content production, and client onboarding. These are the workflows that consume the most repeatable hours, break most often when done manually, and produce the clearest ROI when automated properly. If your agency bills £800k per year and your team spends 2 days per month per client on reporting alone, you are burning 15–20% of your capacity on formatting data someone already has.

I work with agency founders who have already tried the obvious solutions. Zapier flows. Make scenarios. ChatGPT for first drafts. These tools work for simple tasks. They fall apart at scale, and nobody maintains them when they break. This post covers what to automate, how to build it properly, and what the results look like.

### Key Takeaways

- Client reporting automation saves 2+ days per client per month for a typical 8-person agency.
- Content production pipelines reduce first-draft time by 60–70% without sacrificing quality control.
- Client onboarding automation cuts the time from signed proposal to active project from 5 days to same-day.
- Zapier and Make work for simple triggers but break under real operational load.
- A full workflow automation build costs £5,000–£8,000 at a fixed fee, delivered in 2–4 weeks.

### Why Zapier and Make Are Not Enough

I am not dismissing these tools. They are useful for simple trigger-action sequences. Send a Slack message when a form is submitted. Add a row to a spreadsheet when an email arrives. Fine.

The problem comes when agencies try to build operational workflows on them. A reporting pipeline that pulls data from Google Analytics, Facebook Ads, and a CRM, then formats it into a branded PDF and emails it to the client on the first Monday of every month — that is not a Zapier flow. That is a system.

Zapier flows break silently. Nobody notices until a client emails asking where their report is. There is no error handling, no fallback, no monitoring.

### Workflow 1: Client Reporting

**The problem:** Your team spends 2 days per month per client pulling data from multiple platforms, formatting it into a branded report, writing commentary, and sending it. For an agency with 10 clients, that is 20 days per month.

**What I build:** An automated reporting pipeline that pulls data from your analytics platforms, formats it against your branded template, generates narrative commentary using AI, and delivers the draft to your team for a 15-minute review before it goes to the client.

**The result:** 2 days per client per month drops to 30 minutes. Your team reviews and approves instead of building from scratch.

### Workflow 2: Content Production

**The problem:** Your agency produces blog posts, social content, email copy, and ad creative for multiple clients. The first draft stage is where most time is spent, and it is the most repeatable part of the process.

**What I build:** A content pipeline where briefs generate structured first drafts automatically. The system uses the client's brand guidelines, tone of voice, and previous content as context. Your team receives a draft that is 60–70% there, ready for human editing.

**The result:** First-draft time drops by 60–70%. A blog post that took 3 hours to draft takes 45 minutes to review and refine.

### Workflow 3: Client Onboarding

**The problem:** When a new client signs, it takes 3–5 days before active work begins. Access requests, platform credentials, kickoff scheduling, project setup, Slack channel creation, template generation.

**What I build:** An automated onboarding sequence triggered by a signed proposal. The system sends access request forms, creates the project workspace, generates initial templates, schedules the kickoff, and sends the client a welcome pack — all within hours, not days.

**The result:** Time from signed proposal to active project drops from 5 days to same-day.

**Build.** I build the systems over 2–4 weeks. Each workflow connects to your existing tools — Google Analytics, Facebook Ads Manager, Notion, Slack, your CRM. Everything is documented.

**Maintain.** A monthly retainer covers system maintenance, new workflow additions, and ROI reporting.

The Retainer: £3,000–£6,000 per month. Maintenance, new workflows, monthly reporting.

For an 8-person agency billing £800k per year, automating reporting alone recovers the equivalent of a full-time salary.

### What Not to Automate

**Client strategy.** AI can inform strategy with data. It cannot replace your team's understanding of a client's market.

**Creative direction.** AI generates options. Humans make creative decisions.

**Relationship management.** Your clients hire your agency because they trust the people. Keep the human touchpoints human.
