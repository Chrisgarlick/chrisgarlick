# Competitive Analysis

## Direct Competitors

### AI Implementation Consultancies (UK-focused)

| Competitor Type | Examples | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|----------------|----------|-----------|------------|
| **Boutique AI consultancies** | Faculty AI, Deeper Insights, Peak AI | Established brand, enterprise case studies, team depth | Enterprise-focused, long sales cycles, expensive (£50k+ projects), overkill for SMBs |
| **Solo AI consultants** | Numerous on LinkedIn | Low overhead, personal relationships, sector knowledge | Typically advisory-only (no build), rely on third-party tools, no proprietary IP |
| **AI-focused dev agencies** | Potato, Reason Digital, various Upwork agencies | Can build custom software, team can scale | Generic positioning, no sector focus, high day rates, no ongoing operational ownership |
| **Automation specialists** | Make/Zapier consultants, "AI automation" freelancers | Low price point, quick delivery, easy to understand | Shallow — connecting SaaS tools, not building systems. Fragile. Limited to what the platforms allow |

### Key direct threat: The "AI Automation Agency" wave
There is a growing cohort of solo operators and small agencies positioning as "AI automation experts" — typically building Zapier/Make flows and calling it AI implementation. They compete on price (£1k-3k projects) and speed. They are NOT the same offer as Chris, but prospects may conflate them initially.

**Chris's counter:** Process proof. Show architecture diagrams. Show the CMS running. Show the audit platform. The depth of tooling immediately separates him from someone connecting Zapier triggers.

## Indirect Competitors (How Prospects Currently Solve the Problem)

| Solution | Why they use it | Why it fails |
|----------|----------------|--------------|
| **Doing nothing** | "AI isn't proven yet" / fear of change | Competitors adopt AI, margin pressure increases, staff churn from repetitive work |
| **Hiring an internal "AI person"** | Control, dedicated resource | Expensive (£60-90k+ salary), hard to hire, takes months to deliver, single point of failure |
| **Big 4 / management consultancy** | Brand trust, board-level credibility | Strategy decks, not systems. £100k+ for a report. No implementation. |
| **Off-the-shelf SaaS** (Clio AI, Xero AI features) | Already in their stack | Limited to what the vendor builds. No custom workflows. Vendor lock-in. Slow to add features. |
| **Their existing IT provider** | Trusted relationship | IT providers do infrastructure, not AI workflow design. Different skill set entirely. |
| **A nephew / junior dev** | Cheap, available | No business context, no methodology, no maintenance plan, disappears |

## Competitive Moat

Chris's moat is not deep yet — he's pre-revenue. But the moat he's building is:

1. **Proprietary tooling** — Kritano for audits, Kritano CMS for content infrastructure. These compound over time.
2. **Sector-specific playbooks** — As he delivers for legal/accountancy/agency clients, each engagement creates reusable workflow templates.
3. **Process proof portfolio** — Every case study shows architecture, not just outcomes. This is hard to fake and impossible to replicate with off-the-shelf tools.
4. **The free audit as a wedge** — Kritano audits are a zero-cost, high-value door opener that no competitor can match without building their own tool.
