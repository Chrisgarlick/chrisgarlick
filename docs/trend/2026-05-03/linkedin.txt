48% of enterprises now call their AI adoption "a massive disappointment."

That's up from 34% last year, according to Writer's 2026 survey of 2,400 global leaders. Meanwhile, 54% of C-suite executives say AI is "tearing their company apart."

Here's what's happening: 93% of developers use AI tools. 97% of executives have deployed AI agents. Adoption isn't the problem. Implementation is.

The data tells a fascinating story. A randomised controlled trial from METR found that AI actually slowed experienced developers down by 19% - despite those same developers believing they were 20% faster. We feel productive. The numbers say otherwise.

But some teams are getting extraordinary results. AI super-users save 9 hours per week - 4.5x more than their colleagues using the same tools.

The difference? Five things: workflow redesign, governance frameworks, proper infrastructure, genuine training, and cross-functional alignment. Not tool selection. Not licence counts.

As Laura Tacho, CTO at DX, puts it: "This is really a management problem."

The companies getting real value from AI are the ones who start by mapping their workflows, not by shopping for tools. They redesign how work gets done, then select tools that fit the new process.

The gap between "AI-equipped" and "AI-integrated" is where most of the value lives. And it's a gap that's getting wider, not narrower.

Is your team measuring AI adoption or AI outcomes? The answer probably explains your results.
