THREAD

1/ 48% of enterprises now call their AI adoption "a massive disappointment."

Up from 34% last year.

The tools aren't the problem. The implementation is.

2/ Here's the data: 93% of developers use AI coding tools. 97% of executives have deployed AI agents.

But only 29% report significant ROI from gen AI. Only 23% from agents.

Near-universal adoption. Marginal returns.

3/ It gets wilder. A randomised controlled trial from METR found that AI actually slowed experienced developers down by 19%.

The kicker? Those same developers estimated they were 20% faster.

We feel productive. The data says otherwise.

4/ But here's the split that matters: AI super-users save 9 hours per week. That's 4.5x more than everyone else.

Same tools. Wildly different results.

The gap isn't the technology. It's how teams integrate it into their actual workflow.

5/ The pattern I keep seeing: companies buy AI tools, hand them to developers, and call it "AI transformation."

No workflow redesign. No training beyond "here's your licence." No measurement beyond adoption rates.

That's not transformation. That's hope.

6/ Laura Tacho, CTO at DX, nailed it: "This is really a management problem."

The five things that actually work:
- Workflow design (not just tool installation)
- Governance frameworks
- Infrastructure investment
- Proper training
- Cross-functional alignment

7/ The companies getting real value from AI aren't the ones with the most tools. They're the ones who redesigned how work actually gets done.

If your AI strategy is "buy Copilot licences and see what happens" - that's why 48% are disappointed.

What's your team's approach?
