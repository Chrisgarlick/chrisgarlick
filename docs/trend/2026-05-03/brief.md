## Key Data Points

- 79% of organisations face AI adoption challenges (double-digit increase from 2025)
- 48% call AI adoption "a massive disappointment" (up from 34% last year)
- 54% of C-suite say AI adoption is "tearing their company apart"
- 93% of developers use AI, but productivity gains plateau at 10%
- METR RCT: AI actually slowed experienced developers by 19%, despite them believing they were 20% faster
- AI super-users save 9 hours/week - 4.5x more than laggards
- Only 29% report significant ROI from generative AI; only 23% from AI agents
- 92% of C-suite cultivating "AI elite" employees; 60% plan layoffs for non-adopters
- 26.9% of production code is now AI-authored
- PR review time increases 91% with high AI adoption; bugs per developer rise 9%

## The Story

Despite near-universal adoption of AI tools (93% of developers, 97% of executives deploying agents), organisations are hitting a wall. The tools work. The implementation doesn't.

The core insight: companies are layering AI onto existing processes instead of redesigning workflows around AI capabilities. Well-structured organisations see AI as a "force multiplier." Struggling ones find AI "exposes existing flaws rather than fixing them."

Laura Tacho, CTO at DX: "This is really a management problem... just trying AI would automatically pay off. But so far, most tools have been used for individual coding tasks."

The five enablers that separate winners from the disappointed: workflow design, governance frameworks, infrastructure improvements, employee training, and cross-functional alignment.
