# IG DM Playbook for @chrisgarlick.ai

The skill produces a list. This file is how to actually send the DMs without sounding like spam.

## The one rule

Every DM has **two parts**:

1. **The hook** (1 sentence). Specific to them. Proves you actually looked at their account.
2. **The body** (1-3 sentences). Templated. Adapted from one of the slots below.

Never send a body without a hook. A bare template reads as a mass DM in three seconds.

## How to write the hook in 30 seconds

Open their IG. Scroll the last 3 to 5 posts. Find one detail with a **proper noun** in it. A name, a place, a product, a launch, a number, a collab. Reference it directly.

Good hooks (specific):

- "loved the Marshmallow Top in pistachio, that drop sold out fast"
- "the Tynemouth Light Blend is a great-looking pack"
- "saw you took on the rewire job at the chapel conversion last week"
- "@bevstephenson, the new salt + honeycomb bar is genuinely the best thing I've eaten this month"

Bad hooks (generic, smell like spam):

- "love what you're doing"
- "great content"
- "your work is amazing"
- "I've been following you for a while"

If you can't find a specific detail in 30 seconds, **skip that prospect**. The yield isn't worth the time.

## DM rules

- **Lowercase.** All-lowercase reads as a friend texting. Sentence case reads as a sales rep. (Capitalise proper nouns and the start of new sentences only when it would feel weird otherwise.)
- **Short.** Under 50 words total. IG truncates anything longer with "more..." and the reply rate halves.
- **No emojis.** Overused, dates the message.
- **No links in turn 1.** Instagram demotes DMs containing links. Save the URL for the reply.
- **No "Hi [Name], I hope this finds you well."** That's a cold email opener. It tells them this is templated.
- **One question OR one value drop.** Not both. Pick the angle.
- **Cadence.** Send 5 to 10 per day max from a single account. New accounts (the @chrisgarlick.ai relaunch) should start at 3 to 5 per day for the first two weeks to avoid IG limit flags.
- **Spread the times.** Don't send 10 DMs in one batch. Send 2 to 3 morning, 2 to 3 afternoon, leave the evening alone.

## The curiosity question library

The templates above each have ONE curiosity question. That's fine when you've got nothing to go on, but in practice you'll often see something on their feed or website that hints at a more specific pain point. Match the question to the signal.

**How to use this:** scroll their IG + website for 60 seconds. Look for posts about hiring, launching, hitting a milestone, opening hours, behind-the-scenes, "we're so busy", "team grew by X", etc. Each is a signal. Pick the question from the section below that matches the signal you saw, and slot it into Slot 1's place in any template.

The question is what makes the DM feel like a real human noticing a specific thing about their business, not a templated mass-send.

### A. Operations + team + onboarding

Use when their feed has: "new team member", "we're hiring", "behind the scenes", training day photos, SOP/process posts, "another busy week" posts.

- how does your team onboard new starters right now? documented process or more tribal knowledge?
- when someone new joins, how long before they're properly up to speed on your systems?
- what's the most repetitive thing in your week that never quite gets standardised?
- how do you currently train new hires on your tone of voice / brand standards?
- if you could clone one person on your team for their process knowledge, who?
- where in the business does institutional knowledge live that would be a nightmare if that person left tomorrow?

### B. Client + customer work

Use when their feed has: client wins, testimonials, "another happy customer", project handovers, intake/enquiry posts.

- how do you currently handle new client intake? form, call, both?
- when a new enquiry comes in, who triages it first and what does that take?
- what does your typical client onboarding look like, week 1 to week 2?
- how do you handle the gap between someone enquiring and someone actually paying?
- what's the most time-consuming part of taking on a new client?
- how are you currently following up with customers who've gone quiet?
- what does post-project handover look like for you right now?

### C. Reporting + data + analytics

Use when their feed has: stats/results posts, "Q1 numbers", growth milestones, dashboard screenshots, channel-mix posts.

- how do you handle monthly performance reporting right now? Klaviyo + GA4, or custom dashboard?
- where does your time go each month - data pulling or commentary writing?
- how do you currently track which marketing channels are actually driving revenue?
- what data lives in spreadsheets that you wish lived somewhere smarter?
- how are you handling pulling info out of PDFs / forms / supplier docs?
- when you do a monthly review, what's the bit that takes longest to pull together?

### D. Content + marketing + comms

Use when their feed has: campaign launches, blog drops, email previews, podcast episodes, "we just shipped" posts.

- how do you currently write your monthly emails / posts / newsletter?
- when you publish something new on one channel, how does it make it to the others?
- what's your current process for keeping voice consistent across content?
- how are you handling content repurposing right now? from one piece to many?
- is content production a solo job for you or does it pass through multiple people?
- where does your content get bottlenecked? ideation, writing, or scheduling?

### E. Admin + finance + ops housekeeping

Use when their feed has: "month-end vibes", invoicing posts, MTD/HMRC mentions, "back-office Friday", anything that hints at admin overload.

- what's eating time on the admin side that has nothing to do with the actual work?
- how do you handle invoice processing and chasing? manual or automated?
- where's your team's biggest admin bottleneck right now?
- is bank reconciliation / expense categorisation still a manual job for you?
- how are you handling MTD prep / quarterly returns at the moment?
- what's the bit of month-end that you dread most?

### F. Sales + lead handling

Use when their feed has: "DM us for prices", quote-related posts, "fully booked", waitlist mentions, lead-gen campaigns.

- how do you currently handle inbound enquiries? form, DM, both?
- when a lead comes in, what's the gap between first contact and a quote going out?
- how are you keeping track of who's where in your sales pipeline right now?
- when you're fully booked, what happens to the next 10 enquiries that come in?
- how do you handle quote generation? templated or written from scratch each time?

## How to pick the right question fast

1. Open their IG. Glance at the last 3-5 posts.
2. **What signal is loudest?** Hiring? Client wins? Launches? Quote requests? Month-end?
3. Match the signal to a section above (A through F, or the matching industry).
4. Pick the question that lands most naturally given what you saw.
5. Combine with your specific [HOOK] sentence + the template body.

If you see no clear signal, default to the operations question: *"what's the most repetitive thing in your week that never quite gets standardised?"* - it works for every business shape.

---

## When they reply

This is the moment that matters. 80% of the value of cold DM is the second message.

The first DM was about THEM. The second DM can finally be about YOU - but only because they invited it by replying.

**If they answered the curiosity question** (Slot 1):

- Respond to what they actually said first ("ah yeah, Klaviyo + GA4 is the most common setup I'm seeing").
- THEN drop the relevant link or context: *"happened to write the recent piece on exactly this: chrisgarlick.com/article/ai-reporting-automation-agencies. third layer is the bit most agencies skip."*
- Leave the next ball in their court. Don't ask for a call yet.

**If they said yes to the free offer** (Slot 2):

- Don't get clever. Get them to send the thing.
- *"perfect. send over [a recent report / your current intake form / a link to your dashboard] and i'll come back with one thing worth automating + ballpark."*
- Deliver fast. Aim for same-day or next-morning turnaround on the audit. Speed is the differentiator.

**If they ignore you for 7+ days**: don't bump. Move on. Bumping a cold DM is the single biggest signal-killer.

**If they reply with "thanks but no thanks"**: reply once, warm, no ask. *"all good, appreciate the reply - good luck with [their thing]."* Closes the loop, leaves the door open for later.

**A note on the blog link**: the AI Reporting Automation article is a good turn-2 send when reporting comes up in conversation. It is NOT a turn-1 send. If you find yourself wanting to lead with "i wrote a piece on...", stop. That's content marketing pretending to be outreach. Lead with a question about them, not a request for their attention.

## What NOT to send

A list of phrasings that immediately mark you as a cold-DM bot:

- "Hi {first name}, I hope this finds you well"
- "I came across your profile and was impressed"
- "I have a quick question for you"
- "Let me know a good time for a 15-minute call"
- "Can I send you our deck?"
- Any sentence ending with three or more emojis
- Any DM longer than 60 words
- Sending a Calendly link in the first message
- Following up within 48 hours
- Asking "did you see my last message?"

---

## Personal hook quality bar

A hook is good if a friend of the prospect would read it and not be able to tell whether it's from a stranger or from you. Specific detail + warm tone = passes the test.

If you can't write a hook that passes that test in under 60 seconds, the prospect isn't worth DM'ing today. Skip and come back when you have a fresh angle (e.g. they just launched something new).
