# One Framework, Six Months of Content

How to take one consulting methodology and turn it into a half-year content calendar. Blog, LinkedIn, email, short-form video, SEO landing pages. The prompts, the cadence, the sequencing.

This is the system I use with consultant clients who have a strong framework, deep delivery experience, and zero time to write about either of them.

The core idea: your methodology is the product. Content is how that product reaches the market. If your methodology is good, twenty pieces of content from it should all be true, all be useful, and all sound like you. The job isn't to invent content. It's to extract it.

---

## The starting asset

You need one document. A clear write-up of your methodology in your own words. Not slides. A markdown or Google Doc covering:

1. **The problem** it solves (one paragraph)
2. **The core principles** (three to six numbered principles, with one paragraph each)
3. **The steps or stages** (the actual sequence you walk clients through)
4. **The common failure modes** (where it goes wrong, what to do about it)
5. **The proof** (one or two anonymised client outcomes, with numbers if you can)

Length target: 2,000 to 4,000 words. If you can't get to 2,000 your methodology isn't crisp enough yet. If you're past 4,000 you're rambling.

This document is the seed. Everything else extracts from it.

---

## What you'll produce from it

A realistic six-month calendar from one strong methodology:

| Format | Quantity | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Blog posts | 6 to 8 | Roughly every three weeks |
| LinkedIn posts | 24 to 26 | One per week, plus reactive |
| Email newsletter | 6 to 8 | One per blog cycle |
| Short-form video scripts | 18 to 24 | Three per blog post |
| SEO landing pages | 3 to 5 | Front-loaded in month 1 |
| Lead magnet or mini-course | 1 | Built in month 1, refreshed in month 4 |

That's roughly 60 to 70 distinct pieces of content from one methodology doc. Each one true, useful, and unmistakably yours. Each one referencing the same underlying framework so the audience sees the through-line.

## The six extraction prompts

These are the prompts I use to turn the methodology document into the content calendar. Treat them as templates. Run each one with your own methodology pasted in.

### 1. Blog post extraction

```
You are a senior content editor working with a consultant whose methodology
is pasted below. Your job is to identify the eight strongest blog post angles
that this methodology generates.

For each angle:
- Headline (must include the keyword the consultant would want to rank for)
- One-sentence promise to the reader
- Three sub-headings the post would cover
- The single most counter-intuitive insight from the methodology that the post leads with

The audience is [audience description]. Reading level: senior practitioner.
No buzzwords. Specific over generic. Each blog post should stand alone
without referencing the others.

Methodology:
[paste methodology document]
```

### 2. LinkedIn extraction

```
You are a LinkedIn content strategist working with a consultant whose
methodology is pasted below. Generate 26 LinkedIn posts, each in the
consultant's voice.

Each post must:
- Lead with a hook in the first line (visible above the "see more" cut)
- Be 100 to 200 words
- End with either a specific question to the audience, or a single
  practical action they can take this week
- Pull from a specific part of the methodology, not be generic

Mix angles across the 26 posts: stat/observation (5), contrarian take (5),
practical tip (5), client story (5), framework explainer (3), industry
commentary (3).

Voice notes: direct, technical, never uses "leverage" or "synergy". UK English
spelling. Short sentences carry weight. Vary length but lean shorter.

Methodology:
[paste methodology document]
```

### 3. Email newsletter extraction

```
Generate 8 email newsletter editions from the methodology below. Each one
should:
- Have a subject line in the consultant's voice (no clickbait, specific
  reader benefit, under 50 chars)
- Be 300 to 500 words
- Centre on one principle or stage from the methodology
- End with one clear ask (reply with X, book a call, share, forward)

The cadence is one email roughly every three weeks. Sequence the eight so
they build on each other — early ones explain the problem, middle ones go
deep on principles, later ones cover failure modes and proof.

Methodology:
[paste methodology document]
```

### 4. Short-form video script extraction

```
Generate 24 short-form video scripts (Reels, TikTok, Shorts) from the
methodology below. Each script must be:
- 30 to 60 seconds when read aloud at a natural pace
- Have a hook in the first 3 seconds (the first sentence must do the work)
- End with a single line of advice the viewer can act on today
- Be three paragraphs maximum, written for spoken delivery, not reading

Mix formats:
- 8 "did you know" insight videos (lead with a counter-intuitive stat or claim)
- 8 "common mistake" videos (lead with the wrong thing most people do)
- 8 "do this instead" videos (lead with a specific alternative)

No music suggestions, no on-screen text suggestions, no production notes.
Just the spoken script.

Methodology:
[paste methodology document]
```

### 5. SEO landing page extraction

```
For the methodology below, identify the three to five distinct niches or
verticals the methodology applies to most strongly. For each, produce an
SEO landing page brief:

- Primary keyword (long-tail, UK-focused, commercial intent)
- Three to five secondary keywords
- H1, H2 structure (six to eight H2s)
- The angle that makes this version of the methodology specific to this
  niche (not just "for X firms" tacked on the front)
- The single proof point that's most credible to this audience
- The right CTA (book, download, audit, contact)

Methodology:
[paste methodology document]
```

### 6. Lead magnet extraction

```
Design a single lead magnet from the methodology below. It should:
- Be downloadable in 5 minutes of reading
- Solve one specific problem the methodology addresses
- Establish the consultant's authority without giving away the entire system
- End with a clear path to the paid engagement (audit, scoping call, project)

Output:
- Title (specific, benefit-led)
- One-paragraph promise
- Full outline (sections + 2-3 bullet points per section)
- Sample of the opening 200 words to confirm the voice is right

Methodology:
[paste methodology document]
```

## The cadence that compounds

You don't ship all 60+ pieces in week one. You ship on a cadence the audience can keep up with.

**Week 1.** Lead magnet live. First SEO landing page live. First blog post published. Three LinkedIn posts.

**Weeks 2 to 3.** One LinkedIn post per weekday. First email newsletter goes out. Three short-form videos.

**Week 4.** Second blog post. Second SEO landing page. Three more videos. LinkedIn continues at one per weekday.

**Months 2 to 6.** Steady cadence: one blog every three weeks, one email every three weeks, one LinkedIn per weekday, three videos per blog. Lead magnet refreshed in month four.

The compounding effect kicks in around month three. By month six you have a content library that someone landing on a single blog post finds five more relevant pieces from. That's the whole game.

## What to do yourself, what to automate

The temptation is to automate everything. Don't. The model that works:

**Always you:**
- The methodology document (it's the source of truth, must be yours)
- The voice editing pass on every piece (5 to 10 minutes per piece to make it sound like you)
- All replies to comments and DMs
- The actual consulting delivery

**AI does the heavy lifting:**
- First drafts of every format
- The mechanical extraction (six prompts, six outputs)
- The repurposing (blog → video script, blog → LinkedIn)
- The SEO research (keyword expansion, related queries)

The split is roughly 80% AI, 20% you. The 20% is what makes it not sound like AI. Skip it and the whole system reads as generic.

---

## When this stops working

The system breaks when:

- Your methodology stops evolving. Six months of content from one framework is fine. Eighteen months gets repetitive. Develop the next layer of the framework or shift focus.
- You start posting just to post. If you're shipping content with nothing new to say, the audience disengages faster than they engaged. Better to pause than to coast.
- The audience grows past what the framework speaks to. If you started with a niche frame and now your followers are broader, the framework needs to broaden too, or you need to focus.
