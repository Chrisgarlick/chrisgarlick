# The Zero-Team Agency Playbook

How to build an AI-enabled agency that delivers like a five-person team, without hiring one.

This is the operational stack, the templates, and the pricing maths I use with solo-founder agency clients. The model is not "AI replaces your team." It's "you + a thoughtful stack does the work a five-person team used to."

The shape of work changed in 2025. Onboarding documents, ad copy, funnel pages, reporting and outreach all moved from "needs a human" to "needs a human reviewer at the end of an automated pipeline." For solo founders, this changes the maths on starting an agency completely.

---

## The shift in plain numbers

Old model. Five-person agency team (junior strategist, copywriter, account manager, ad ops, sales setter). Loaded cost in the UK including tools, software, employer NI and overhead: £8,000 to £12,000 a month.

New model. One founder plus an AI stack. Same outputs, slightly slower turnaround on edge cases, much faster turnaround on standard work. Loaded cost: £200 to £500 a month in tool subscriptions.

The work itself doesn't get cheaper. The overhead does. That's the entire business case.

Margin that used to fund five salaries now sits with the founder. Or, more usefully, it funds the early years when you're under-charging on purpose to land the first ten clients.

---

## The stack

The toolkit changes every six months but the structure doesn't. Six categories, each with one primary tool.

### 1. Onboarding and contracts

One tool that takes a discovery call transcript and produces the welcome pack: signed contract, scoped statement of work, kickoff schedule, comms template. Claude or GPT with a custom system prompt, fed via a markdown template you fill once and clone forever.

### 2. Ad and funnel copy

Variants at scale, not just first drafts. The system prompt produces ten ad variants per campaign brief, including hook variations, CTA variations and audience-specific framings. Run them all, kill the losers fast, keep the winners.

### 3. Funnel pages and landing copy

The same prompt structure produces the long-form landing page, the short-form mobile version, the email opt-in version. One brief, three formats, ten minutes.

### 4. Reporting

The boring monthly report becomes a five-minute job. Connect the data sources, pipe the numbers through a template, AI writes the insights paragraph, you check the numbers haven't done anything weird, and ship.

### 5. Cold outreach

Personalised first lines at scale. The agent reads the prospect's website or LinkedIn, writes one specific opening sentence per prospect, slots it into a sequence. Reply rates on this beat generic templates by 3x to 5x in 2026.

### 6. Booking and follow-up

Calendly or similar, plus a follow-up sequence written once and triggered automatically. The "no reply yet" follow-up at day three, the "haven't heard back" at day ten, the "closing the loop" at day twenty-one. All automated.

## The onboarding template

This is the asset that compounds the most. Build it once, refine it every five clients, and your "I've got a new client starting Monday" moment becomes a 30-minute job.

Structure:

1. **Brief intake form** the client fills before kickoff. Eight to twelve questions. Goals, audience, deliverables, brand assets, calendars, access. Nothing should be guessed at later.
2. **Auto-generated contract and SoW** produced from the brief plus your standard terms.
3. **Welcome email sequence** that introduces you, sets expectations, and tells them what's happening in week one.
4. **Kickoff meeting agenda** generated from the brief, with the right questions for their specific situation.
5. **Slack or shared workspace** spun up with the right folders and the right access already configured.

Cycle time for client number one: a day. For client number two onwards, with the template in place: thirty minutes.

## The delivery model

The mistake most solo agency founders make is delivering bespoke for every client. Bespoke doesn't scale. The right model is:

- **Productised core** — the thing every client gets, exactly the same way, every time. This is what the AI stack delivers.
- **Bespoke layer on top** — your judgment, your client-specific recommendations, your strategy. This is your high-leverage hour.

Roughly 80% of delivery should be productised. 20% bespoke. If it's flipped the other way, you're building a job, not a business.

## Reporting cadence

The number-one thing that makes a solo agency look amateur is bad reporting. Inconsistent format, late, missing context, no narrative.

The cadence:

- **Weekly:** a short Slack update from the agency to the client. Auto-generated from this week's metrics, edited by you in five minutes. Three lines: what happened, what's next, what we need from you.
- **Monthly:** the proper report. Branded, in their colours, with insights not just numbers. AI drafts it from the data. You add the strategic context. Twenty minutes total.
- **Quarterly:** the actual conversation. Done in person or on Zoom, no slides necessary. The numbers go in an email afterwards.

## The outreach cadence

If you're starting from zero clients, you need to be doing personalised cold outreach for the first six months. Not generic sequences. Specific to the prospect, sent at a sustainable cadence.

The model:

1. Build a list of 200 ideal prospects. Same vertical, same operating model, same approximate size.
2. The agent enriches each row with one specific personalisation note: something true about their business that a real human would notice.
3. Send 10 to 15 per day. Not 200. Burnout pace is also throttle pace.
4. Three-touch sequence: opener, follow-up, closing-the-loop.
5. Reply tracking goes into a simple spreadsheet or CRM. Anyone who replies gets a real human conversation, not another automation.

UK note: under PECR, B2B cold outreach has a "legitimate interest" basis as long as the recipient could reasonably expect contact about your services. Don't scrape consumer data, don't ignore unsubscribe requests, and don't use a sender domain that looks like a one-shot mailer. The compliance overhead is real but manageable.

## When this breaks (when to actually hire)

The model is not "never hire anyone." It's "don't hire until the maths obviously demands it."

The signals that it's time:

- You're routinely doing 60-hour weeks and the bottleneck is bespoke client work, not productised delivery.
- A single client is 40%+ of revenue (concentration risk; a hire reduces this by freeing you to chase the next).
- A specific repeatable task (e.g. video editing, account management) is consuming 20+ hours a week and a £30k-£40k specialist would clear it.

When you do hire, hire for the highest-leverage repeatable task. Account management before strategy. Production before strategy. Strategy is the last thing you hire for, and only when you genuinely don't want to do it any more.

## The first 90 days

Week 1 to 2. Build the stack. Don't sign clients yet. The onboarding template, the ad copy prompt, the reporting prompt. Test each one on a fake client to make sure the outputs are good.

Week 3 to 4. Land client number one through your existing network or a single referral. Charge them less than you should. Use them to stress-test the stack. Fix everything that breaks.

Month 2. Land clients two and three. Don't lower the price further. Iterate the stack based on what client one taught you.

Month 3. Start cold outreach. By now your case study from client one is real. You're targeting clients four to ten.

By month six you should be at £5k to £8k MRR. By month twelve, £10k to £20k MRR. If you're well behind those numbers it's the offer or the pricing, not the work.
