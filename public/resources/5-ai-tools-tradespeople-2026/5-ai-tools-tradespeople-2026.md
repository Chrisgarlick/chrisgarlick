# 5 AI Tools Every Tradesperson Should Use in 2026

No marketing agency. No copywriter. No studio. Five tools that handle the bits of running a trade business that always get pushed to the bottom of the list. All from your phone, in under thirty minutes a week.

The honest version of this: you don't need to be techy. You don't need to learn anything complicated. Most of these tools are free or cost a few quid a month. The hard part isn't the tools. It's setting them up once and then letting them run.

By the end of this you'll have:

1. A way to make a video of every job in five minutes
2. A weekly Google post that goes out without you doing anything
3. A review request that fires every time you finish a job
4. A quote follow-up sequence that chases for you
5. Seasonal campaigns ready to fire when the right time of year hits

Total monthly cost done right: £20 to £40. Total time to set it all up: a Saturday afternoon, spread over two weeks.

## Tool 1: Phone-to-Reel app for before/after videos

**What it does:** turns the phone photos you already take (before and after each job) into a 15-second video that's good enough to post on Instagram, TikTok or Facebook Reels.

**Why this one first:** before/after videos are the single best content a trade business can post. They show your actual work. People save them, share them, send them to mates. The algorithm pushes them. One good Reel a week from a roofer, plumber or builder beats every fancy marketing thing put together.

**The tool (2026):** CapCut. Free, on your phone, has built-in templates designed for before/after content. You drop in two photos (before and after), it does the transition, the music, the text overlay, all of it.

**How to use it:**

1. Before you start a job, take three photos. Wide shot, close-up of the problem, the area you'll be working on.
2. When you finish, take three photos from the same angles.
3. In the van before you drive off, open CapCut. Pick a "before / after" template. Drop your photos in. Add one line of text ("Boiler swap — Stockport — same day"). Done in three minutes.
4. Post straight to Instagram and Facebook. Use 3 to 5 hashtags max — local ones, not generic.

**What good looks like:** one Reel per finished job, posted same day. Twenty Reels a month if you're doing five jobs a week. After three months you'll have a body of work people scroll through and trust before they even call.

## Tool 2: AI text writer for posts and replies

**What it does:** writes Instagram captions, Facebook posts, Google replies and email follow-ups for you. You talk to it, it writes.

**The tool (2026):** Claude or ChatGPT on your phone. Free version is enough to start. £15-20/month for the paid tier when you're using it daily.

**How to use it:**

The trick is one good prompt that you save and reuse. Save this in your notes:

```
You are writing a social media post for a [your trade] in [your area].
Voice: direct, plain English, no marketing nonsense. UK English.
Post this caption alongside the photo I describe:

[describe the job in your own words — voice note transcribed if quicker]

Output:
- One caption, 60 to 100 words
- One question to ask at the end so people comment
- 5 location-specific hashtags
- No emojis at the start
```

Voice note to your phone for 30 seconds describing the job. Paste the transcript into the prompt. Get back a caption that sounds like you, not a marketing agency.

Same prompt works for:
- Google Business posts (just ask for "Google Business post, 200 chars")
- Replies to your Google reviews (ask for "polite, brief, mentions the area")
- Follow-up emails on quotes

**What good looks like:** five minutes a day, talking to your phone, end up with everything you need to post that day.

## Tool 3: Google Business Profile (and the post scheduler)

**What it does:** your Google Business Profile is the most important thing you own online. It's what shows when someone searches "[trade] near me." Posting weekly on it improves where you show up in those searches.

**The tool (2026):** the Google Business Profile app on your phone. Plus a scheduler if you want to batch posts in advance — Buffer or Hootsuite do this for free at the volume you need.

**How to use it:**

1. Claim your Google Business Profile if you haven't yet. Verify it by post or video call.
2. Fill out everything. Hours, services, photos, the works.
3. Post once a week. Doesn't have to be fancy. A photo from a job + 2 sentences + one call to action.
4. Use Tool 2 to write the post. Use Tool 1 to make a 15-second video to attach. Schedule it via Buffer for Tuesday or Wednesday mornings.

**What good looks like:** four Google posts a month, every month, with a photo or short video on each. Google rewards consistency. After six months you'll notice you're showing up higher when local people search for your trade.

**One thing not to skip:** reply to every single Google review within 48 hours. The replies count more than people think.

## Tool 4: Automatic review requests

**What it does:** every time you finish a job, the customer gets a short message asking for a Google review. You don't have to remember. You don't have to ask awkwardly. It just happens.

**The tool (2026):** the easiest version is a Tally form linked to an email automation. £0 to £5 a month. The fancier version is something like NiceJob (about £40/month) which does it all for you, but you don't need it day one.

**How to use it (the cheap version):**

1. Build a simple form on Tally. Two questions: "How did it go on a scale of 1 to 5?" and "Email address."
2. When you finish a job, send the customer a one-line WhatsApp or text: "Thanks again — would you mind taking 10 seconds to fill this in?" with the form link.
3. If they pick 4 or 5, the form auto-sends them an email with the Google review link.
4. If they pick 1 to 3, you get an alert and can call them yourself.

This is the highest-value automation in the pack. Every Google review compounds. Tradespeople with 50+ reviews show up first in local searches. Tradespeople with 5 reviews are buried.

**What good looks like:** ten new Google reviews a month if you're doing twenty jobs a month. Two thirds of customers will leave a review if you ask. Most never get asked.

## Tool 5: Voice-note-to-text (for everything else)

**What it does:** you talk to your phone in the van between jobs, it turns it into text. That text becomes social posts, quote emails, follow-up messages, whatever you need.

**The tool (2026):** Apple Voice Memos (built in, free, transcribes automatically now), or Otter on Android. Plus Tool 2 (Claude/ChatGPT) to turn the transcripts into whatever format you need.

**How to use it:**

The pattern is "drive between jobs, talk into the phone, sort it out at the end of the day."

- Drive to the next job → record a voice note about the one you just finished, the photos you took, what you'd want people to know. 1 minute.
- At the end of the day → run the transcripts through Tool 2's prompt. Get back a caption, a quote, an email, whatever.
- 15 minutes of admin at the end of the day handles everything.

**What good looks like:** zero time spent sitting at a laptop writing. All the social, all the follow-ups, all the quote emails come out of voice notes between jobs.

## The order to set them up

Don't try to do all five at once. Pick the one that solves your biggest problem first.

**Most leaky pipeline → start with Tool 4 (review requests).** Every job that ends without a review is money walking away. Set this up in week one.

**Patchy social presence → start with Tool 1 (Reels) + Tool 5 (voice notes).** Set up the format. Run it for two weeks. Then add Tool 2 for captions.

**Quote response rate is poor → start with Tool 2 + an email automation for follow-ups.** The day-three "just checking" email gets more replies than people expect.

**You're already busy but not showing up in Google searches → start with Tool 3 (Google Business).** This is the slowest to compound but the biggest long-term win.

## What NOT to do

**Don't try to be on every platform.** Pick one. Instagram or Facebook. Get good at one before you add the next.

**Don't post AI-written stuff without reading it.** AI captions sometimes sound off. Read every one. Change two words. Make it sound like you.

**Don't use stock photos or AI-generated images.** Your before/after photos beat anything fancy. People want to see your actual work, not a stock photo of a boiler.

**Don't pay for ads in the first three months.** Get the organic stuff running first (Reels, posts, reviews, Google Business). Ads work much better once you have proof on your profile.

**Don't outsource any of this in the first six months.** The reason this works for solo tradespeople is the voice. An agency posting "we provide professional plumbing services" loses every time to a real video of you fixing a real boiler with one sentence about it.
